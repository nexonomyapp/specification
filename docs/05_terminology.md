# Nexonomie — Terminology

The following terminology should be used consistently throughout the application.

## Asset / Entity

A geographically identifiable object that participates in the territorial system.

The preferred internal term is **Asset**, because it emphasizes that the object is represented, inspected and reused by the platform.

In user-facing scientific text, **Entity** may be used when emphasizing systemic relationships.

## Asset Type

The semantic category of an asset.

Example: TREE, BUILDING, BUSINESS, POND.

## Project

The overall urban or territorial development study.

## Study Area

The geographic territory considered by the study.

## Intervention Area

The specific geographical area directly modified by the project.

The distinction between Study Area and Intervention Area should be preserved.

## Scenario

A possible state or intervention plan associated with a project.

## Intervention

A project-specific operation such as removing, adding, modifying or affecting an asset.

## Dependency

A relationship through which the state or activity of one entity can influence another.

Dependencies may be geographic, economic, social, environmental, infrastructural or other types.

## Dependency Path

A sequence of dependencies connecting two assets.

## Propagation

The process of exploring dependency paths starting from directly affected assets.

## Effect

A measurable or qualitatively describable consequence associated with a change in an entity or dependency.

## Ecosystem Service

A beneficial function or contribution provided by an ecosystem or natural entity to human society or the environment.

## Reference

A documented source supporting a parameter, relationship, model or valuation.

## Valuation

The transformation of an effect into a comparable quantitative or monetary measure.

## Nexonomic Analysis

The systematic analysis of direct and indirect consequences of an intervention through a network of dependencies.

## Nexonomic Cost

A final aggregate measure of the consequences of an intervention, according to the formal Nexonomie methodology.

This term should not be implemented as a simple sum of arbitrary monetary effects. The aggregation methodology must be formally defined first.
